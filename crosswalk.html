AI Benar crosswalk (beta): the EU AI Act and the NIST AI RMF

Version 0.1, 28 November 2026. How the EU AI Act’s obligations for high-risk AI systems line up with the four functions of the US NIST AI Risk Management Framework. A crosswalk is a side-by-side map between two sets of rules, showing where their requirements match, where they partly overlap, and where they don’t.

Not legal advice. This is a working map to help you plan, built from the official texts. Whether and how the AI Act applies to your system depends on the facts; take legal advice before relying on it.

What the NIST AI RMF is

The AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, published January 2023) is a voluntary framework from the US National Institute of Standards and Technology. It organises AI risk management into four functions. Govern runs through the other three:

Govern

Policies, roles, accountability and a culture of managing risk, including risks from other companies’ AI.

Map

Understand the context: what the system is for, who it affects, and what could go wrong.

Measure

Test and track: methods and metrics for trustworthiness, and monitoring over time.

Manage

Act on what you find: prioritise and treat risks, and respond to and recover from incidents.

When the high-risk obligations apply

The Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 July 2026, moved the dates. Obligations for high-risk systems listed in Annex III, such as AI used in employment, education or credit decisions, now apply from 2 December 2027. Those for AI built into products covered by Annex I, such as medical devices, apply from 2 August 2028. The transparency rules in Article 50 stayed largely on their original schedule.

What the NIST framework doesn’t cover

Some AI Act obligations are legal steps with no equivalent in a voluntary framework. They need their own plan:

  • Article 43: Conformity assessment before the system reaches the market
  • Article 47: An EU declaration of conformity
  • Article 48: CE marking
  • Article 49: Registration in the EU database
  • Article 22: An authorised representative in the EU, for providers outside it
