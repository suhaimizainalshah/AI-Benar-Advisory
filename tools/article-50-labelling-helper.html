Worked example — Scenario ① in words

A reporter drafts a council-meeting report with an AI tool. An editor checks the facts, rewrites two paragraphs, and the named editor takes responsibility for publication.
The helper asks: text → deployer → AI-assisted → public interest: yes → human review with editorial responsibility: yes.
Result: the Art. 50(4) exception applies. AI-generated text on matters of public interest normally must be disclosed — unless it has undergone human review and a person holds editorial responsibility. No label is legally required, but the helper still issues the audit record and offers an optional good-practice transparency note.
Contrast with Scenario ③: an AI avatar testimonial resembles a real customer who never said those words. That's a deepfake under Art. 50(4) — disclosure is mandatory, no editorial-review exception saves it, and the label must appear before or at first exposure.

Which Article 50 duty does what

DutyWhoWhat the helper generates
Art. 50(1) — AI interactionDeployer of a chatbot / AI system people interact withNotice that the user is interacting with AI — unless that is already obvious from the circumstances.
Art. 50(2) — Machine-readable markingProvider of a generative AI systemMarking snippet so outputs are detectable as artificially generated or manipulated, in a machine-readable format.
Art. 50(4) — DeepfakesDeployer generating / manipulating image, audio or video resembling real people, places or eventsDisclosure label, timed for first exposure; lighter existence-only wording for artistic / fictional / satirical work.
Art. 50(4), 2nd part — Public-interest textDeployer publishing AI-generated text informing the publicDisclosure label — or, where human review + editorial responsibility applies, a documented exception record. (Art. 50(5) then governs timing: information must be clear and distinguishable at first exposure.)
