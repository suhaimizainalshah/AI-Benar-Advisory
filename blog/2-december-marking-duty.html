The 2 December deadline: machine-readable marking for AI-generated content

28 November 2026 · Suhaimi Zainal Shah, AI Benar Advisory

Here’s a deadline that has been sitting in plain sight since July, and plenty of the teams it catches still haven’t done the work: 2 December 2026.

That is the deadline if your company provides a tool that generates images, video, audio or text, and it was on the EU market before 2 August 2026. In that case you must add machine-readable marking to its output by 2 December 2026, under Article 50(2) of the AI Act, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the Digital Omnibus on AI). From then on, the transparency duty applies in full: outputs must be marked in a machine-readable format so they can be detected as artificially generated or manipulated.

Two things worth getting straight

First, “machine-readable” is not the same as a visible label. Think of it this way: a visible label is a name badge — it tells the person in front of you. Machine-readable marking is the barcode underneath — it tells every system that handles the file afterwards, whether or not a person reads the badge. In practice that means metadata or a standard marking such as Content Credentials (C2PA), or a page-level marking like the one our free AI Hakikat tool generates for you. A caption alone does not do it.

Second, the duty sits with the provider of the generative AI system — the party that built and put the system on the market — not with every person who types a prompt. If you only publish AI-generated content, different disclosure rules may apply: deepfakes must be disclosed as artificially generated or manipulated, and AI-generated text on matters of public interest must be disclosed too. That is the deployer’s side, and it has applied since 2 August 2026. That distinction matters, because it is the one most teams get wrong — they assume the duty follows the prompt, when it follows the product.

The practical check, before 2 December

  1. Do we provide a generative AI system that was on the market before 2 August 2026? If yes, is its output marked in a machine-readable format?
  2. Do we publish AI-generated content in the EU? If yes, is the disclosure visible where a person will actually see it?
  3. Can we prove it? Keep a dated record of what was marked, how, and when — an audit trail beats a memory.

This is a deadline, not a cliff edge you discover afterwards. The systems caught by it have been on the market for months, the grace period was four months, and the marking is a defined piece of work. The fines for getting transparency wrong run to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. It is better done in November than explained in December.
